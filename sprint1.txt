# 15. Join safety

This is one of the best additions from the latest Sprint 1 document.

Suppose:

```text
Orders → Customers
```

is many-to-one.

Safe.

But:

```text
Orders → OrderItems
```

may create duplication.

So calculate:

```text
source uniqueness
target uniqueness
fan-out factor
duplicate-key rate
expected cardinality
observed cardinality
```

Then:

```text
SAFE
SAFE_WITH_AGGREGATION
RISKY
UNKNOWN
```

This prevents one of the most dangerous BI errors:

> **Correct-looking KPI with incorrect joins.**

The proposed join-safety model in the latest specification is exactly what we should use.

# 48. Most important architectural rule

There are now **three different concepts**, and we should never mix them.

### KPI definition

> What does this number mean?

### KPI monitoring

> Is the number behaving unusually?

### Entity analysis

> Tell me about Product A.

They are different.

```text
KPI Definition
     ↓
KPI Monitoring
     ↓
KPI Investigation
     ↓
Targeted Entity Analysis
```

And again:

> **Entity analysis is not entity anomaly detection.**

We only perform deeper entity statistics when the entity becomes relevant through contribution, promotion, or explicit user request.

## Bottom line

**Yes, I would update Sprint 1 to include KPI Governance.**

The cleanest interpretation is:

> **Sprint 1 = establish the company, data, security, documents and governed KPI definitions.**

Then:

> **Sprint 2 = make those approved KPIs intelligent.**

And within the later intelligence layer:

> **KPI-level anomaly detection is broad; entity-level analysis is selective.**

That gives us a stable foundation without accidentally building the expensive “analyze every entity” system you are correctly trying to avoid.

The latest uploaded specification is already a strong foundation for the source/data side; the only major change I recommend is moving **KPI registration, proposal, validation and approval into this Sprint 1**, while keeping actual KPI monitoring and investigation out of it.
